# Compounding beats intensity

> [!abstract] The idea in one sentence
> Small actions repeated consistently outperform large actions performed rarely, because consistency compounds and intensity does not.

## Explanation
Intensity is a one-time payment; consistency is an annuity. Reading 20 pages a day is 7,300 pages a year — roughly 25 books — while a heroic weekend of reading produces one. The difference isn't effort per session, it's the number of sessions. The same shape holds for saving money, training, writing, and learning: the exponent on time dominates the coefficient on effort.

Intensity also has a hidden cost — it requires motivation, and motivation is unreliable. Consistency can be made to require almost nothing, which is why it survives bad weeks.

## Why it matters / where it applies
- Choose the habit you can do on your worst day, not your best one
- Judge systems by whether they survive a bad week, not by their peak output
- When behind, resist the urge to "catch up" heroically — just restart the streak

## Evidence & examples
- 1% better daily ≈ 37x over a year; 1% worse daily ≈ near zero
- Savings rate compounding beats occasional windfalls for most people
- A vault of one note per day beats a weekend of frantic note-taking

## Counter-argument / when this breaks
Compounding assumes the input actually accumulates. Some domains genuinely reward intensity: creative breakthroughs often need long uninterrupted blocks, and some opportunities are one-shot and time-boxed. Consistency at the wrong thing compounds into wasted years — direction beats both. And low-intensity practice can plateau; deliberate practice requires periodically working at the edge of ability.

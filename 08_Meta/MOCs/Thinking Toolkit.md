# 🧰 Thinking Toolkit

Prompts to run against any note, problem, or decision. Getting smarter is mostly having better questions on hand.

## Understanding something new
- Can I explain this to a 12-year-old? *(Feynman)*
- What is this **not**? Where's the boundary?
- What did people believe before this idea existed?
- What has to be true for this to be true?
- What's the simplest version? What's the most extreme version?

## Evaluating a claim
- What's the strongest argument **against** this? *(steelman)*
- Who benefits if I believe this?
- What evidence would change my mind? If none — it's a belief, not a conclusion.
- Am I confusing *correlation*, *causation*, and *coincidence*?
- Is this true, or does it just sound good? *(fluency ≠ truth)*

## Making a decision
- Is this reversible? Reversible → decide fast. Irreversible → decide slow.
- **Inversion:** what would guarantee failure here? Now avoid that.
- **Second-order:** and then what? And then what after that?
- **Regret minimization:** at 80, which choice do I regret less?
- What's the cost of *doing nothing*? (Usually underrated.)
- Am I choosing this, or avoiding something else?

## Solving a problem
- **First principles:** what do I know is definitely true? Rebuild from there.
- Where's the actual bottleneck? Optimizing a non-bottleneck does nothing.
- Has someone already solved this? (Usually yes.)
- Can I make the problem smaller instead of the solution bigger?
- What if I had to solve it in 1 hour? What if I had a year?

## Reviewing myself
- What am I doing out of habit that I'd never choose fresh today?
- Where am I busy instead of effective?
- What am I avoiding, and what is the avoidance costing me?
- If a friend described my situation to me, what would I tell them?
- What did I get wrong recently — and was the *reasoning* wrong or just the outcome?

## Core mental models worth internalizing
| Model | One line |
|---|---|
| Compounding | Small consistent inputs beat rare heroic ones |
| Opportunity cost | Every yes is a no to everything else |
| Inversion | Solve the problem backwards |
| Circle of competence | Know the edge of what you know |
| Second-order thinking | Consequences have consequences |
| Map ≠ territory | Your model is not reality |
| Occam's razor | Prefer the explanation with fewer assumptions |
| Hanlon's razor | Don't assume malice where incompetence suffices |
| Pareto (80/20) | Most output comes from few inputs |
| Antifragility | Some things gain from disorder |
| Via negativa | Improvement by removal, not addition |
| Loss aversion | Losses hurt ~2x more than gains please |
| Survivorship bias | You only see the winners |
| Confirmation bias | You find what you look for |
| Sunk cost | Past spend is irrelevant to the next choice |
